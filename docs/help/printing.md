# Printing: sizes, page and dpi

Set these in the **Output** panel, under **Print**. The **PNG for print** and **PDF for print** exports use them, and the **Paper** preview shows them.

## Print size

Pick a size from the list:

- **ISO A paper**: A0 to A6. A4 is the common office sheet in most of the world; each size is half of the one before it.
- **ISO B paper**: B3, B4, B5, used for some posters and books.
- **US paper**: Letter, Legal, Tabloid (11 × 17 in) and Super B (13 × 19 in).
- **Photo prints**: 4 × 6, 5 × 7, 8 × 10, 11 × 14 and 12 × 12 inches.
- **Posters**: 12 × 18, 16 × 20, 18 × 24 and 24 × 36 inches, and 50 × 70 and 70 × 100 cm.
- **Clothes and small things**: a T-shirt chest print (30 cm), a full T-shirt front (12 × 16 in), a left-chest print (4 × 4 in), a 3 × 3 in sticker and a business card. These fill the whole area, with no page margin.
- **Custom size**: type any width and height in millimetres, centimetres or inches.

## Page and angle

- **Page**: **Fit the art** turns the page to suit the artwork: wide art gets a landscape page, tall art a portrait page. You can also choose **Portrait** or **Landscape** yourself.
- **Turn the art on the page**: any angle from −180° to 180°, or one of the quick buttons. The artwork is made as large as fits on the page at that angle. The turned art shows on the Paper preview and in the PDF for print. PNG files and the CMYK PDF keep the art straight, so turn the picture in your printing program if you need a turned PNG.
- **Page margin**: the white border around the art, in millimetres. **Auto** uses 6% of the short side of the page, and no margin for the clothes and small things sizes.

## What dpi means

dpi means dots per inch. Here it means how many pixels of your artwork go into each inch of paper. SungFluence works out the pixel size from the print size and the dpi. For example, a 12 × 16 inch print at 300 dpi needs 3600 × 4800 pixels.

Only the number of pixels counts. The dpi number stored in a file does not make it sharper. A screen ignores dpi altogether.

The dpi of your printer, often 1200 or more, is something else: it is how finely the machine places its ink dots. You do not need to match it.

## Which dpi to choose

| dpi | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| 72 or 96 | Screens only: websites, social posts, slides. The pixel size is what matters there, so set the **Artboard width** instead. |
| 150 | Large posters and banners seen from a few steps away, about 1.2 to 2 m (4 to 6 ft). Also the usual minimum for printing on T-shirts. |
| 200 to 250 | A middle choice for larger posters when you are not sure how far away people will stand. |
| 300 | Anything seen up close: photo prints, flyers, cards, books and magazines. Most print shops ask for this. |
| 600 | Only when a printer asks for it, for example for some one-color line art. For photo prints and T-shirts, more than 300 dpi only makes the file larger. |

You can type any value from 30 to 2400 dpi in **Any dpi**.

### A note for text art

Text art is made of small letters, so the dpi decides whether people can read them. The Print section shows the letter size in points for grid styles such as ASCII. Below about 4 pt, the letters read as texture rather than words. Choose a larger print size or fewer characters if you want them readable.

### The largest size SungFluence draws

Print exports are at most 9000 pixels on their longest side. If a size and dpi need more, for example A0 at 300 dpi, the Print section tells you the dpi that the export will really have.

## Always check with your printer

These are common guidelines, not rules. Printing method, paper and viewing distance all change what looks sharp. When it matters, ask the printer or the print service what they need.
