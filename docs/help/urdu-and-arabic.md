# Writing in Urdu and Arabic

SungFluence comes with two fonts for Arabic script: **Noto Nastaliq Urdu**, the flowing style used for Urdu poetry, and **Noto Naskh Arabic**. Both work offline.

Arabic script joins its letters. If letters were placed one by one, they would lose their joined shapes. So in the styles below, SungFluence places whole words, from right to left.

## Styles that support it

| Style | How |
| --- | --- |
| Calligram | Whole words fill each line of the shape, right to left. The shape word can be in Urdu too, and can have several lines (press Enter). |
| Text portrait | Rows of whole words, right to left. Each word takes the photo's color under it. |
| Spiral and Rings | Whole words around the spiral, reading anticlockwise with the tops of the letters outward. |

Other styles place letters one at a time, so Urdu does not join in them yet.

## Tips

- Paste your text into the **Text** box. Line breaks are fine: in a calligram they become a pause, and **Mark between lines of the text** (under More controls) can add an Urdu full stop (۔) or a small ornament where a verse line ends.
- Text in Arabic script uses Nastaliq on its own, whatever the Typeface says. To use Naskh instead, pick **Naskh (Arabic, Urdu)** as the typeface.
- Nastaliq is tall, so SungFluence adds room between lines for it.
- **Writing** (under More controls in Calligram) can force right to left or left to right, for mixed text.

## An idea: a verse in a portrait

1. Open a portrait.
2. In the Text art panel, choose **Text portrait**, or **Calligram** with the shape set to **Photo**.
3. Paste the verse into **Text**.
4. Set **Color** to **Photo**, and try light paper and dark paper in the Output panel.
5. Export **PNG for print** or **SVG with live text**.
